Let's go over my experience on creating a studio. I'd have to warn you because you're gonna see a lot of bad decisions.

## How I started

The thought of having something of my own has always been there. I started out by wanting my own minecraft server. That failed due to my inexperience. It was back in 2021. So I gave up the hope of having a server of my own and decided to do freelance work.

I worked as a freelancer for the past 5 years. Years of working for different people, I had success but the feeling of wanting something of my own was always there. I was tired of working for teams that made more money using my content than I did. I had better ideas, I knew how to create stuff so why would I keep working people? Why would I be in the worry that if I fail a team, I would be discarded? I wanted something more permanent and hence in 2025 I decided to take a leap. I again started to work towards a server of my own. A lot of planning, by a lot I mean a lot and whole less execution.

> Mistake#1: I dreamt too big while not working on a foundation and growing it.

I worked on a minecraft plugin for months yet it still wasn't finished. Version 1 was discarded, version 2 was half built, I relied on some people, got disappointed from time to time. If you don't work with a professional you shouldn't expect too much. Dreaming big is fine as long as you execute. Execution was my problem. I thought too much and drifted from one thing to another. But I learnt a lot back in 2025 working on that plugin. I gained a lot of technical experience. But life will always have a ways to surprise you. I hit a wall after my father passed suddenly in June, 2025. My server work was stalled. I wasn't exactly depressed but my mental health was suffering, I was still processing what had happened. I had no fallback after that. Everything was my responsibility and no one was there to save me. I had to find work and break into whatever I had saved through out the years I've freelanced. Mind you that I had stopped taking frequent commissions back then so I had to start working again. Again, there was my anxiety problem. I froze when I failed to meet expectation of people. I distracted myself with this and that.

After going through all that, in December, 2025 I decided that I would start my team "One Raid Studio". I initially wanted to break into Hytale because it was going release in January. I worked towards a plan on what I should do moving on. And that decison was also a mistake.

> Mistake#2: I discarded my half built server and decided to move on to a completely different game. A game I had no experience with. A game that was completely new and had no chart on data on if it will succeed on not.

## The Trap

Gambling is addictive. I gambled a lot with my decisions. I was a "Let's gamble it" kind of guy, if you know what I mean. And therefore I started my "Hytale Journey"!

I recruited three of my friends who were completely beginners and had no idea about my niche or enough experience.

> Mistake#3: I took in people who had no prior interest in my niche or any background of any kind.

You simply cannot entrust people with tasks who don't know what they want in their life. You shouldn't work with people who just says "yes" to you that they'll work. You should ALWAYS work with people who had prior interest, experience or is already engaged. And oh god do I have bad news for you that if you think you'll be able to train people to fit into your work. No. You can't. Never.

They had nothing to show. I even taught them Java from scratch which they completely forgot after a month. It was very easy for me to get into Hytale Plugin development because I understood stuff quickly. But for them even a tutorial along with rounds of explaining didn't get stuff into their head. I was acting as a teacher and not a studio owner. I wasn't working on growing a studio, I was mentoring people. My next mistake is:

> Mistake#4: I tried to teach and mentor people. The time I've taught them is the time I could've used to build something for my studio.

Now I understand why companies bother so much with the hiring process. Losing time training someone is losing time making money. That is why the selection process is so strict that companies make sure that the person they're recruiting will give them better ROI than the invested time in training. I would've progressed more if I invested that time into something else.

But I will admit that I became really good friends with the people that I spent my time mentoring and teaching. They are some of the best people I've met so far in my life. But as someone who has everything on their shoulder, I should've invested my time creating friendship through general socialization rather than "training" them to work for you.

My trap was that I thought, things will work out. Deadlines will be met. They will learn eventually but people have a life of their own. They are not out there to help you achieve what they desire. They are creatures with their own desire and goal as well. A simple nod or yes from them doesn't mean they will achieve things for you. It simply means that "I'll be there but as soon as something better comes in my path, I'll simply choose that.". And why wouldn't they? They don't owe you any favor or should be held accountable for their desire of personal benefit.

## How I fumbled

If you've come this far reading then I probably did entertain you with my story or simply cause the word "fumble" caught your attention. Regardless, if you've paid attention so far then you know already where I've fumbled. It's not recruiting people and training them rather it's the part where I changed my niche completely. And now you know what I'm back at again? Minecraft.

I never did finish the plugin for hytale which I was supposed to release back in May, 2026. I never did finish the other 3 plugins that I planned out after the first one. I never did even play hytale properly. Hytale's gameplay never did impress me. It wasn't supposed to be cause it was still not a game that was fully released rather a game that was being built slowly with the help of community.

Switching over to minecraft again, isn't actually a mistake. Rather it was the right move in my opinion. Another problem I struggled with is that:

> Mistake#5: I jumped around from one idea to another. Didn't complete what I started first.

I'm a knucklehead who jumps into a pit of spikes despite knowing it will definitely hurt. I can't get something into my head completely without experiencing it even though it feels like a completely bad idea.

One of my team members was cut off because of their miscommunication and lack of progress. He is a good friend but he is terrible at putting in actual work. It is baffling to me that some people are so "busy" that they don't even have an hour of the day to put in work for personal growth. If you're wondering where they are putting their time, I would say check their screentime. Two of the people I hired was like that. 8+ hour screen time and they call themselves busy. The friend who I cut off, later (after 5-6 week to be specific) had a conversation with me that they're trying for X job cause they need money. I was feeling empathetic and decided to give him another chance. And this time I decided to teach him something completely new.

> Mistake#6: I re-opened a hole that was already sinking my boat and this time I decided to make a bigger hole instead.

It's alright to be empathetic and it is human. But I believe you need to be logical and stern when it comes to building something. They were not in immediate need or huge trouble yet I still took them back into the team and decided to completely teach them something different. I decided to teach that friend modeling which I was my main work. He showed a lot of promise and good progress in that. But hey, you are a guy who has 5 years of experience in a field and now how can you rely on a person that is completely new to a niche to get you things done? That was very stupid of me. I ended up spending more time fixing his stuff rather than getting things done. I thought to myself, "Hey I'm making another person work for me, he can do it so why should I?". It was very lazy of me to think that. The thing I assigned him took him hours while it would've taken me 10-15 min to do. I even paid him hourly for it from my own pocket. Funny thing is he asked for a raise shortly after some tasks was done. I simply have no words.

> Mistake#7: I dumped the task that I can do better and more quickly on other people.

Even though I had to put in my own time into the task, I still would've get it done quickly, get things done absolutely perfectly to my liking and get exactly what I needed. Yet I dumped things like doing research on other people.

> Mistake#8: I asked non experts to do research on a field that I should've researched instead. As a founder I should research primary things alone. That is a better way to retain information and learn rather than reading a report created by others.

By this point six months have already passed and it was june. My studio shifted from a four person team to a solo founder on the fourth month when everyone else failed to learn things in time. I just realized it much later.

Out of three people I've recruited, only one person I have been able to rely on so far. Special thanks to my friend Nafees Munsarim. He atleast had the passion to do things and a drive for creating stuff. I still assign him this or that today.

I'll conclude this section after telling you about my next mistake.

> Mistake#9: I paid people to do things I could've done myself faster and better.

Am I a millionaire? Hell no. I'm a half orphan and a student with a bit of a saving. I burnt through that saving from January to August. I did very very little work even though I had so many opportunities. My ego was so big that I gave up some very good opportunities over my reckless ambition. I paid people just so they have the "motivation" to do things for me. I wasn't the boss, I was literally begging people to do things for me which I could've done myself. I've been so so lazy that the hole in my bank account was the trigger to open my eyes.


## How I'm recovering

Being a founder isn't sexy or a flex. It just means that you're a person who has no issues screwing up again and again. Failure is a greater part to starting something of your own than success. And that is fine, I knew the risks but I did it anyway. I learnt so many things from the mistakes that I did and moving on in life they will be surely helpful.

I'm working on creating products for MCModels and resumed taking in commissions again while working slowly alone to build my studio.

I applied at MCModels on August 31st and as I'm writing this now I just got a email that my application was rejected due to lack of stuff in my portfolio. Hey another road-block, just great!

I will admit that they journey so far has been great and really eye opening for me. Along the journey, I fixed my laziness, learnt how to execute things properly, fell in love again with a very sweet girl, made few great friends along the way and built a hell a lot of stuff.

Will I give up on my ambition of creating something of my own? Absolutely no. I still have the luxury to able to fail so I will keep failing until I make something finally work. Do I know what I am doing? Maybe, debatable. Will I be more responsible with my studio? Absolutely. Will I be try to recruit or hire people for my studio? Not now or until I can actually hire a professional for tasks I am not the best at.

Thank you for reading so far. Please reach out to me if you had a similar experience and how you recovered from it.

## Lessons I've learnt along the way

For those who just wants to know the mistakes:

1. Create a foundation first and release it instead of creating the perfect product.
2. Grow on what you are best at and don't leave it unless there is a lack of opportunity in that niche or you have a better opportunity at another niche.
3. Never work with people who have no idea at all about what you do.
4. Don't try to teach or mentor people the basics. Let people teach themselves.
5. Don't jump around with ideas. Complete what you started first. No matter how sexy the next idea looks, write it down and leave it in your notebook. Execute and complete your intial idea first, release and if it doesn't work out then move on to the next.
6. Don't go back to things that didn't work out in first place.
7. If you can execute something faster and better than another person, do it yourself. The other person being faster is not enough, quality should always be prioritized.
8. Do your own research please.
9. Don't pay people for things you can do yourself.

> Don't just note down your mistakes. Start addressing them as well.
